##  About the Project

The **CPI Inflation Analysis in India** project explores Consumer Price Index (CPI) trends across Rural, Urban, and Rural+Urban sectors from 2013 to 2023.

The project uses Microsoft Excel to clean and transform data, analyze category-wise price index trends, compare annual and monthly patterns, study pre- and post-COVID changes, and investigate correlations between fuel prices and other expenditure categories.

##  Project Objectives

- Analyze CPI trends from 2013 to 2023.
- Compare price index trends across Rural, Urban, and Rural+Urban sectors.
- Study year-on-year changes in the General Index.
- Analyze monthly trends in food categories.
- Compare CPI trends before and after COVID-19.
- Examine fuel and transportation price index movements.
- Perform correlation analysis between fuel and selected expenditure categories.
- Present findings through Excel-based analysis and visualizations.

##  Tools & Techniques

##  Key Findings

### 1. Broader Category wise Inflation

The purpose of this analysis is to examine the distribution of CPI data observations across broader expenditure categories.

<img width="647" height="391" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/91ce47c7-ad8a-4ffc-9f3b-6d6d35925ac5" />

**Food overwhelmingly drives the index at 44.44%, proving that basic sustenance dictates overall inflation trends. Combined with Luxury (14.81%) and Clothing (11.11%), these top three categories command over 70% of the entire basket, revealing that consumer spending is heavily concentrated in specific lifestyle and essential areas.**


### 2. Year-on-Year Analysis

The annual average General Index for the Rural+Urban sector increased from approximately **129.20 in 2016 to 177.62 in 2023**, indicating an overall rise in the consumer price index level.


### 2. Sector-Wise Comparison

The analysis compares Rural, Urban, and Rural+Urban index values to identify differences in price trends across sectors.

  <img width="604" height="369" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/4fce57d9-0f2a-4935-8ff0-d0a74de6c946" />

**Inflation peaked in 2022, driving urban rates to a high of 7.0%. This spike was fueled by the Russia-Ukraine conflict, which triggered global energy crises, supply chain shocks, and domestic food price spikes. By 2023, rates dramatically halved to ~3.2%, signaling aggressive monetary corrections and a strong, successful economic cooldown.**


### 3. Food Inflation Analysis

Monthly and annual comparisons examine categories such as cereals, milk, vegetables, fruits, pulses, and other food items to identify variations in their index values.


  <img width="861" height="368" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/47b9932b-4b02-42d6-b7e1-fe55c573c872" />

**Food inflation trends show sharp volatility in vegetables, peaking in October 2022 (+4.0%) and plunging in December 2022
 (-12.7%). Fruits saw the highest surge in February 2023 (+7.1%). Overall, October 2022 marked the highest inflation, while December 2022 recorded the lowest, reflecting seasonal supply shocks.**

### 4. Pre- and Post-COVID Analysis

The project compares trends in food and beverages, household goods and services, and health from 2013 to 2023 to understand how their index levels changed over time.

<img width="687" height="338" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/b17408ec-8ee2-4382-b061-75f5f4afce7c" />

**Lockdown disruptions and panic buying caused Food inflation to double to 8% in 2020, up from 4% in 2019. Conversely, Household services slumped to a low of 2% due to restricted mobility, while Health inflation remained stable at 4% before surging to a historical peak of 8% in 2021.**

### 5. Fuel and Transportation

The workbook examines the relationship between fuel and light and transport and communication index values.

<img width="757" height="371" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/4bf25466-4efe-4fbc-9a78-f45433628162" />

**The dashboard reveals a strong 66% positive correlation between fuel and transport inflation. This significant relationship proves that fuel price hikes directly drive transport costs upward, showing that volatility in energy markets immediately spills over into commuting and logistics expenses for consumers.**

### 6. Correlation Analysis

The workbook reports the following correlations:

<img width="552" height="332" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c41f2951-afac-47e1-9710-82bc91113a8c" />

| Category Comparison | Correlation |
|---|---:|
| Fuel vs. Non-alcoholic Beverages | 0.689 |
| Fuel vs. Transport and Communication | 0.662 |
| Fuel vs. Food and Beverages | -0.199 |
| Fuel vs. Cereals and Products | -0.239 |

These results indicate positive associations between fuel and non-alcoholic beverages, and between fuel and transportation. The other two comparisons show weak negative associations in the analyzed data.

*Note: Correlation does not imply causation. CPI index values are not the same as inflation rates; percentage changes in the index over a defined period are needed to measure inflation.*
